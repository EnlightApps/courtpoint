2. Summary

  • The app keeps everything on your watch: scores, match times, match history and settings. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else, and none of it goes into Android backups (section 3).
  • We have no server and no user accounts, and the app contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking code.
  • CourtPoint is a paid app, bought through Google Play before you install it. The app contains no purchase or billing code.
  • The personal data we receive comes from outside the app: the order records Google Play shows us for each sale (section 4), emails and other messages you send us (section 6), the closed test and promo codes (section 7), and the reviews and crash reports Google shows us (section 13).

3. Data stored on your watch

The app stores, on your watch: your match settings, so the next match starts the same way; the match in progress, saved after every point so it can resume and deleted when the match ends; the results of up to 200 finished matches, with scores and durations but no dates or times of day, each with a random identifier used only to select or delete it; and app preferences such as haptics and whether a match is in progress. It uses Android's standard on-device app storage (DataStore and SharedPreferences). Other apps cannot access it, the app never sends it anywhere, and we never receive it. You can delete history entries in the app at any time, and everything is deleted from the watch when you delete the app.

Backups. CourtPoint excludes all of its data from Android backup and from device-to-device transfer, so none of it is copied into your backups or to a new watch.

4. Purchase

CourtPoint is a paid app ($3.99, or the equivalent price in your local currency), sold through Google Play before you install it, under Google's terms and privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). Google Play order records

Play Console also lets us find an order by its order number or by the buyer's full email address, and refund it. We do not try to identify buyers from these records, and we do not download them.

9. Your rights

Under KVKK (Turkey)

You have the rights in Article 11 of KVKK (https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/MevzuatMetin/1.5.6698.pdf). How to use them is described below.

Under the GDPR

You have the right to access your data (Art. 15), to have it corrected (Art. 16), to have it erased (Art. 17), to restrict its processing (Art. 18), to data portability (Art. 20), to object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21), and not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing (Art. 22). We make no such decisions.

Under the Swiss FADP

You have the right to information about your data (Art. 25), to data portability (Art. 28), and to have your data corrected, deleted or its processing stopped (Art. 32). We answer within 30 days (FADP Art. 25(7)).

Right to object

You can object at any time, on grounds relating to your situation, to processing based on our legitimate interests (sections 4, 6, 7 and 13). Email us and we will stop, unless we have compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or need the data to establish, exercise or defend legal claims. You can also ask us for the balancing test behind each legitimate interest.

How to exercise them

Email support@enlight-apps.com, or write to the postal address in section 1, in English or Turkish. We may ask you to confirm that the email address is yours. We answer free of charge, in writing (by email, or by post if you wrote by post), stating what we did, or why we refused and that you can complain to a data protection authority (section 10) or go to court:

  • within 30 days under KVKK (KVKK Art. 13(2));
  • within one month under the GDPR (extendable by two months for complex requests, in which case we tell you within the first month, with the reasons for the delay).

Because the app keeps your match data on your watch and never sends it to us, we cannot access, export or delete it for you. You control it directly: delete history entries in the app, or delete the app.

10. Complaints

You can complain to a data protection authority:

  • EU and EEA: a data protection authority, in particular in the EU/EEA country where you live or work, or where you think the GDPR was breached (list: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/about-edpb/members_en)
  • Switzerland: you can report to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC, https://www.edoeb.admin.ch)

11. Children

CourtPoint is a sports scoring app for a general audience and is not directed at children. The app collects no personal data from anyone, including children. If we learn that a message comes from someone under 18, we reply once if needed, then promptly delete the message and the contact details instead of keeping them for the period in section 6. If we learn that a closed-test sign-up comes from someone under 18, we remove them from the test and delete what we hold about them. A parent or guardian can also ask us to delete such data, and we will.
