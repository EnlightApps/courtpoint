Privacy Policy

CourtPoint — Effective and last updated: October 22, 2026

This policy covers the CourtPoint app for Apple Watch, the CourtPoint app for Wear OS, and the CourtPoint website (all pages at https://enlightapps.github.io/courtpoint/, in English and Turkish). It is written to meet the information requirements of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, Articles 13 and 14), Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 (KVKK, Article 10) and other privacy laws that may apply to you. A Turkish translation is available; if the two versions differ, this English version prevails, except that for the KVKK information notice (aydınlatma metni) the Turkish text prevails.

1. Who is responsible

CourtPoint is developed and sold by Aydın Akyol, an individual developer publishing under the name EnlightApps (EnlightApps is not a registered company). He is the controller (veri sorumlusu) for the personal data described in sections 4, 6, 7 and 13.

  • Contact for anything in this policy, including requests to exercise your rights: support@enlight-apps.com
  • EU representative (GDPR Art. 27): none appointed. We rely on the exemption in Art. 27(2)(a) for processing that is occasional and unlikely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms.
  • Data protection officer: not required (GDPR Art. 37).
  • Turkey: exempt from registration in the Data Controllers' Registry (VERBIS) under KVKK Board Decisions 2025/1572 and 2025/2393.

2. Summary

  • The apps keep everything on your watch: scores, match times, match history and settings. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else. On Apple Watch, app settings can also be in your own device backups (section 3).
  • We have no server and no user accounts, and the apps contain no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking code.
  • CourtPoint is a paid app on both platforms, bought through the App Store or Google Play before you install it (section 4 says who the seller is). The apps contain no purchase or billing code and send nothing.
  • The personal data we receive comes from outside the apps: the order records Google Play gives us for each Wear OS sale (section 4), emails and other messages you send us (section 6), the Wear OS closed test and promo codes (section 7), and reviews and crash reports the stores show us (section 13).

3. Data stored on your watch

The apps store the following on your watch, using the platform's standard on-device storage (UserDefaults on Apple Watch; DataStore and SharedPreferences on Wear OS). Other apps cannot access it. The apps do not send it anywhere, and we never receive it. It is deleted from the watch when you delete the app, and you can delete history entries yourself at any time. Copies in your own device backups are described below.

Match settings

Your setup choices, so the next match starts the same way:

  • Sport last selected: tennis, padel, or pickleball (Wear OS)
  • Sets: best of 1, best of 3, or best of 5
  • No-Ad / Golden Point scoring: on or off
  • Match tiebreak: on or off
  • Doubles: on or off
  • Pickleball game target: 11, 15, or 21 points
  • Pickleball rally scoring: on or off
  • Who serves first: you or your opponent
  • Classic court labels (D/A instead of R/L): on or off

Match in progress

Saved after every point so the match can resume if the app closes, and deleted when the match finishes, is ended or is reset:

  • Sport
  • Tennis and padel: points for each side; games for each side in each set; sets won by each side; current set number; whether a tiebreak or match tiebreak is in progress; tiebreak points for each side; who served first in the tiebreak; set format; No-Ad / Golden Point setting; match tiebreak setting; current server; who served first in the match; doubles setting; doubles serving-order position, including at the start of the tiebreak; whether the match is over and who won
  • Pickleball: score for each side; current server; server number (1 or 2); who served first; doubles setting; rally scoring setting; game target; whether the game is over and who won
  • Time played so far, as a number of seconds (no date or time of day)

Match history

Results of up to 200 completed matches, for your own statistics. Each entry contains:

  • A random identifier created on the watch, used only to select or delete the entry (it is not linked to you or your device)
  • Sport
  • Whether you won or lost
  • Match duration in seconds (no date or time of day)
  • Tennis and padel: games for each side in each set; set format; No-Ad / Golden Point setting; match tiebreak setting; doubles setting
  • Pickleball: final score for each side; doubles setting; rally scoring setting; game target

If part of the stored history ever becomes unreadable, the app skips the unreadable entries and does not keep a copy of them; they are removed the next time history is saved.

App preferences

  • Whether you have completed the welcome screen
  • Whether you have seen the gesture hints
  • Haptics: on or off
  • Whether a match is in progress, so the app knows to resume it (Wear OS)

Backups

Backups of your devices, if you use them, are made by Apple's or Google's operating system under their own privacy policies; we cannot access them. Apple states that the Apple Watch backup on your paired iPhone "includes app settings", and that when you back up your iPhone to iCloud or your computer, that backup includes your Apple Watch data (https://support.apple.com/en-us/108358). Copies of CourtPoint's settings, and possibly its other data, can therefore be in your own backups and stay there after you delete the app, until you delete the backup. On Wear OS, CourtPoint excludes all of its data from Android backup and device-to-device transfer.

4. Purchase

CourtPoint is a paid app on both platforms ($3.99, or the equivalent price in your local currency). There is no free trial and no in-app purchase.

  • Apple Watch: sold through the App Store before you install it, under Apple's terms and privacy policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). In Türkiye, the EU, Iceland and Norway, Apple Distribution International sells the app in its own name as our commissionaire; in the United States, Apple Inc. sells it as our agent. Apple's privacy policy names Apple Distribution International Limited as the controller for people in the European Economic Area. Apple gives us only aggregated sales reports (totals by country and currency), with nothing about individual buyers.
  • Wear OS: sold through Google Play before you install it, under Google's terms and privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). In the European Economic Area, Google is the seller (merchant of record), acting as our agent. In Turkey and the United States we are the seller, and Google handles the sale and payment for us as our agent.

The apps contain no purchase, billing or licence-check code. We never see your payment details or your Apple or Google account.

No other party collects personal information about your activity over time or across websites through our pages or apps. GitHub logs IP addresses for its own security, as described above.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR

You have the right to access your data (Art. 15), to have it corrected (Art. 16), to have it erased (Art. 17), to restrict its processing (Art. 18), to data portability (Art. 20), to object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21), and not to be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing (Art. 22). We make no such decisions.

Right to object

You can object at any time, on grounds relating to your situation, to processing based on our legitimate interests (sections 4, 6, 7 and 13). Email us and we will stop, unless we have compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms, or need the data to establish, exercise or defend legal claims. You can also ask us for the balancing test behind each legitimate interest.

Under KVKK (Turkey), Article 11

You have the right to:

  • learn whether your personal data is processed;
  • request information about it if it is;
  • learn the purpose of the processing and whether the data is used for that purpose;
  • know the third parties in Turkey or abroad to whom it is transferred;
  • request correction if it is incomplete or inaccurate;
  • request its deletion or destruction under the conditions in KVKK Article 7;
  • request that corrections, deletions or destructions be notified to the third parties to whom it was transferred;
  • object to a result against you that arises from analysis exclusively by automated systems;
  • claim compensation if you suffer damage because of unlawful processing.

Under other laws

If you live in a country whose law gives you other or additional rights over your personal data (for example a US state), contact us and we will handle your request under that law.

How to exercise them

Email support@enlight-apps.com. We may ask you to confirm that the email address is yours. We answer free of charge, in writing by email, stating what we did, or why we refused and that you can complain to a data protection authority (section 10) or go to court:

  • within one month under the GDPR (extendable by two months for complex requests, in which case we tell you within the first month, with the reasons for the delay);
  • within 30 days under KVKK (KVKK Art. 13). If a request needs extra cost, the fee set by the KVKK Board's tariff may apply.

Because the apps keep your match data on your watch and never send it to us, we cannot access, export or delete it for you. You control it directly: delete history entries in the app, or delete the app (and, on Apple Watch, any backups that hold it).

You can complain to us first, at support@enlight-apps.com. We confirm that we received your complaint within 30 days, look into it, and tell you the outcome.

You can also complain to a data protection authority:

  • Turkey: Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu (https://www.kvkk.gov.tr). Under KVKK you must apply to us first (KVKK Art. 14(2)); you can then complain to the Board within 30 days of learning our answer, and in any case within 60 days of applying (Art. 14(1)).
  • EU and EEA: the authority in your country (list: https://www.edpb.europa.eu)

11. Children

CourtPoint is a sports scoring app for a general audience and is not directed at children. The apps collect no personal data from anyone, including children. If we learn that a message comes from someone under 18, we reply once if needed, then promptly delete the message and the contact details instead of keeping them for the period in section 6. If a closed-test sign-up comes from a child under 13, we do not add the child to the closed test and delete the sign-up. A parent or guardian can also ask us to delete such data, and we will.

12. Security and breaches

We keep the data we receive to the minimum described above. The email account is accessible only to us. If a breach affects personal data we hold, we notify the KVKK Board within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, and the people affected as soon as reasonably possible (directly where we can reach them, otherwise on this page). Under the GDPR, we notify the data protection authority of each EU/EEA country where affected people live within 72 hours, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to them, and tell the people affected directly when the risk to them is high. We keep a record of every breach.

13. Ratings, reviews and crash reports

The "RATE COURTPOINT" button opens CourtPoint's page in the App Store or the Google Play Store. CourtPoint sends nothing when you tap it.

  • Reviews: if you rate or review CourtPoint, Apple shows us your rating, the review title and text, the nickname you chose, the date and time, and the App Store country; Google shows us your rating, the review text, the name on your Google Play profile, the date and time, and sometimes your device and app version details (maker, screen size, Android version, app version, language). Both let us reply publicly on the store.
  • Crash reports: if you allow your device to share diagnostic and usage information with app developers (Apple) or usage and diagnostics data (Google), we may receive reports of CourtPoint crashes on your device, through Xcode (Apple) and Play Console (Google). Apple's reports contain the device model, system version, the date and time of the crash and of the app launch, technical details of the crash, and an anonymised identifier for each device. CourtPoint itself contains no crash-reporting code.
